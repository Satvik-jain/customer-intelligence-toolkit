# Monetization

Use this skill to frame pricing decisions and choose how to test them. Distinguish pricing hypotheses from observed customer behavior.

## Inputs

Identify product value, customer segment, alternatives, cost and margin constraints, usage pattern, purchase role, current pricing, and decision objective (profit, revenue, adoption, retention, or another goal).

## Workflow

1. Define the objective and constraints; revenue, profit, adoption, and retention can point to different choices.
2. Compare customer value, costs, competitive alternatives, usage variability, and willingness heterogeneity.
3. Select candidate structures such as subscription, usage-based, transaction fee, tiering, bundling, or hybrid. See [pricing-methods.md](references/pricing-methods.md).
4. Select a WTP method for the question. Separate stated WTP, choice trade-offs, incentive-compatible elicitation, and real transaction/field evidence.
5. Treat survey or simulated WTP as a hypothesis. Plan a real price test or behavioral validation where feasible; state fairness and trust risks.
6. Compare options on expected economics, uncertainty, operational needs, and customer impact. Do not calculate a demand curve without appropriate data and a defensible model.

## Output

Provide the decision objective, segment, candidate model/tiers, assumptions, evidence and limitations, economics needed, fairness considerations, and validation plan. Use the shared [evidence labels](../../references/evidence-labels.md).

## Limits

Do not present synthetic responses as actual WTP, treat one method as universally most accurate, or apply a universal hypothetical-bias correction. Do not recommend personalized or dynamic pricing without considering customer trust, fairness, and applicable constraints.
