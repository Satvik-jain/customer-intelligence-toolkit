# Choose a method for the question

| Question | Candidate method | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| What problem or context matters? | Interviews, observation, diary or field study | Does not estimate prevalence by itself |
| Can users complete a task? | Moderated or unmoderated usability test | Does not establish demand or willingness to pay |
| How common is a stated attitude? | Structured survey with a defined sample | Self-report, recruitment, and nonresponse bias |
| Which bundle or attribute trade-off is preferred? | Conjoint or discrete-choice design | Hypothetical and model assumptions remain |
| Will a specific change cause a behavior change? | Randomized experiment when feasible | Requires sound assignment, instrumentation, and adequate power |
| What happens in ordinary use? | Analytics, cohort, transaction, or field data | Confounding and selection effects limit causal claims |
| What should be explored before recruiting? | Synthetic simulation | Not actual customer evidence or validated demand |

Methods are complementary, not a universal ranking. Pick based on the decision, risk, feasibility, population, and uncertainty. Use more than one method when the decision depends on both explanation and behavior.

## Source note

Usability testing and online controlled experiments have established methodological guidance; this compact decision table is project-authored synthesis and does not replace a study-specific protocol. See [verified source notes](../../../references/sources.md#customer-research-and-validation).
