# Segmentation

Use this skill to create segments that support a concrete decision such as targeting, product design, positioning, or research recruitment.

## Inputs

Identify the market boundary, decision, available customer data and its provenance, geography, business model, and constraints. Distinguish customer, user, buyer, and payer where relevant.

## Workflow

1. Define the segmentation objective and market boundary.
2. Propose candidate dimensions, prioritizing needs, jobs, behaviors, and observed value where data supports them. Use demographic or geographic fields to describe and reach segments, not as unsupported explanations.
3. Check segment usefulness: identifiable, meaningfully distinct, sizable enough for the decision, reachable, responsive, and aligned with product capability.
4. Describe each segment using observed needs, context, behaviors, alternatives, and constraints. Keep a persona narrative separate from the measurable segment definition.
5. For B2B, define ICP at the account level and distinguish account fit from the roles and buying committee within an account.
6. If using CLV, RFM, or clustering, state data coverage, assumptions, and what the method cannot show. Do not treat cluster labels as customer motives without qualitative support.
7. Propose how to validate segment distinctions and monitor drift.

## Output

Use the [segment scorecard](references/segment-scorecard.md). Include segment definitions, supporting evidence, size/fit considerations when known, uncertainty, exclusions, and the next validation step. Use the shared [evidence labels](../../references/evidence-labels.md).

## Limits

Do not invent segment sizes, imply that a persona represents all customers, or infer sensitive traits without a justified, ethical, and lawful basis. When customer evidence is absent, label segments as hypotheses. Use Customer Simulation only to explore scenarios after the population assumptions are explicit.
