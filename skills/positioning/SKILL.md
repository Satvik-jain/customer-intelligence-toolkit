# Positioning

Use this skill to clarify who an offer is for, what category or alternative it is compared with, what benefit differentiates it, and what makes the claim credible.

## Inputs

Identify target segment, customer problem or job, alternatives, current product capabilities, proof available, and intended channel or decision stage.

## Workflow

1. State the target and the frame of reference.
2. Identify category expectations or points of parity, then candidate points of difference grounded in product capability and customer value.
3. Translate features into customer outcomes and specify reasons to believe.
4. Draft a concise positioning statement and a message hierarchy.
5. Check clarity, relevance, differentiation, credibility, and consistency with evidence. Identify claims that need substantiation.
6. Where alternatives exist, design a real customer message or concept test. Use simulation only for early hypothesis exploration and label it.

## Output

Provide the positioning statement, target, category, parity requirements, differentiated benefit, proof, message options, evidence status, and a test plan. See the [positioning reference](references/statement-and-message-tests.md) and shared [evidence labels](../../references/evidence-labels.md).

## Limits

Do not claim a “white space” solely from a framework map. Do not use unsupported superlatives, fabricated proof points, or simulated testimonials. Emotional/rational appeal and gain/loss framing are context-dependent; test before asserting performance.
