# Consumer Psychology

Use this skill to reason about how context and behavioral mechanisms may shape a customer decision. Apply theories as lenses for questions, not as deterministic descriptions of individuals.

## Inputs

Identify the product or decision, purchase context, decision stage, target group, and the evidence available. Ask for missing context only when it could change the analysis.

## Workflow

1. Map the relevant decision stage: need recognition, information search, evaluation, purchase, or post-purchase.
2. Select only the mechanisms relevant to the question: goals and motivation; perceived risk and trust; emotion; habits and defaults; reference points and framing; social identity and influence; or cultural context.
3. Explain plausible mechanisms in conditional language. Distinguish a mechanism supported by evidence from an untested hypothesis about this audience.
4. Consider competing explanations and situational factors, including alternatives, availability, price, and decision roles.
5. Convert each useful explanation into a question or observable behavior that can be checked with customers.
6. If asked to simulate responses, hand persona construction and synthetic panel work to Customer Simulation.

## Output

Provide the decision context, relevant mechanisms, plausible competing explanations, evidence labels, and testable implications. Use the [decision-factors reference](references/decision-factors.md) when comparing behavioral lenses.

## Limits

Do not infer an individual’s preferences from demographic or cultural membership alone. Avoid universal claims about emotional, social, or cognitive effects. Survey intent is not observed purchase. Synthetic responses are not customer evidence. Follow the shared [evidence labels](../../references/evidence-labels.md).
