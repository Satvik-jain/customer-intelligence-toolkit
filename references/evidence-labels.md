# Evidence labels and source handling

Use these labels consistently in analysis and structured outputs.

| Label | Meaning | Minimum disclosure |
|---|---|---|
| [REAL CUSTOMER EVIDENCE] | Directly observed or recorded customer behavior, statements, or responses. | Source or collection method; sample and date when known; material selection limits. |
| [EXTERNAL RESEARCH] | Findings reported by a named external study or source. | Traceable citation; method, population, date, and scope when available. |
| [SYNTHETIC SIMULATION] | Content generated by an AI model to represent a hypothetical customer or scenario. | Model and setup when known; persona basis; prompt/scenario; whether calibrated against real data. |
| [ASSUMPTION] | An unverified input, constraint, or scenario choice. | State the assumption and how it could change the result. |
| [INFERENCE] | An interpretation or recommendation derived from other evidence. | Identify the inputs and uncertainty; do not restate an inference as an observation. |

## Mixing evidence

- Keep evidence categories separate in tables, charts, summaries, and recommendations.
- A model response conditioned on a real dataset remains [SYNTHETIC SIMULATION]. The source data can separately be [REAL CUSTOMER EVIDENCE].
- Never invent a citation, sample size, customer quote, transaction, or field result.
- If source details are missing, say that the source is not traceable from the available material.
- Do not use synthetic panel size as a real-world sample size or as a basis for ordinary sampling error or confidence intervals.
- Do not claim causality from surveys, interviews, synthetic scenarios, or observational associations alone.

## Source note

These five labels are the toolkit's operational evidence taxonomy. They are intended to prevent category errors in analysis, not to replace a research standard. See [verified source notes](sources.md#project-authored-operational-synthesis) for the distinction between established methods and toolkit-authored workflows.
